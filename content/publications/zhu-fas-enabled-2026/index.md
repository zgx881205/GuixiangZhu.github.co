abstract: Cognitive radio (CR) has emerged as a promising solution to meet the spectrum
  demands of massive sensor networks; however, cooperative spectrum sensing (CSS)
  remains highly vulnerable to spectrum sensing data falsification (SSDF) attacks.
  In this paper, we investigate the physical-layer security of CSS from a fluid antenna
  system (FAS) perspective and propose a robust anti-SSDF framework by exploiting
  spatial adaptability. A cognitive wireless radio network (CWRN) with FAS-enabled
  secondary users is formulated, where each sensor dynamically selects antenna positions
  over a continuous aperture, inducing spatial decorrelation and channel randomness.
  Based on these properties, a spatial-consistency-based defense mechanism and a FAS-assisted
  secure fusion strategy are developed to effectively suppress coordinated and intelligent
  SSDF attacks. To further enhance security, we propose a novel FAS-Machine Learning
  hybrid scheme that integrates deep learning with FAS spatial diversity for adaptive
  attack detection. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed scheme significantly
  enhances sensing accuracy and robustness under various attack scenarios and fusion
  rules, while maintaining low hardware complexity, highlighting the effectiveness
  of FAS as a physical-layer security enabler for CSS. The proposed framework achieves
  up to 25% higher detection accuracy compared to existing methods under cooperative
  SSDF attacks, with only 15% additional computational overhead.
